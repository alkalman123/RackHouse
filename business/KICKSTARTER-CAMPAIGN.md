# Kickstarter campaign: complete launch kit

**The shareable link, live now:**
**https://alkalman123.github.io/RackHouse/kickstarter.html**

Post that link on climbing pages today. It explains the project, shows the
planned rewards and budget, has one-tap share buttons, and collects
"notify me" emails. When your Kickstarter pre-launch page exists, paste its
URL into `SHOP.kickstarter.url` in `js/store-data.js` and every button on
the site switches to sending people to Kickstarter.

Why there isn't a Kickstarter URL yet: a Kickstarter project can only be
created by the person who'll receive the money. Kickstarter verifies
your identity and bank account, so this part is yours (about 45 minutes,
below). Everything you'll paste into it is written out here.

---

## 1. What you need before you start (US)

- 18+ with a US address and a US bank account (checking) in your name or
  the business's name
- Government-issued ID and your SSN, or an EIN if you file as a business
  (free from irs.gov in 10 minutes; see `LEGAL-AND-ENTITY-FORMATION.md`)
- A credit or debit card on file (Kickstarter's verification step)
- **Real photos of real printed prototypes**: print one of each product
  first. Kickstarter requires physical-product projects to show a working
  prototype in its current state and doesn't allow photorealistic
  renderings to stand in for the product. Use the site renders only where
  they're clearly labeled as renders, and lead with real photos.
- **A load test of each printed prototype**: hang the full rack the
  product page lists (for the Gear Board, a double rack, nuts and six
  draws) for a week, shake it, toss the Crag Ring and bars in a pack. The
  capacities on the site come from the CAD, not from a test yet.

### Story (paste into the Story editor; add your photos between sections)

```
I'm a climber in Chicago, and I design and 3D-print gear storage that fixes the small annoyances in my own climbing life. Rackhouse is that shop, and this campaign gets it off the ground.

THE PROBLEM: GEAR THAT FALLS OFF
Hooks, pegs and open notches are fine until the bin gets bumped, the pack gets tossed in the trunk or you walk the rack to the crag. Then a cam slides off and ends up under the seat. So every Rackhouse organizer clips through fully enclosed windows. Once a carabiner's gate closes through one, the only way off is to open the gate again. It took two rounds and 40 prototypes to get every window big enough to clip, with a strip thin enough to clip around.

THE GEAR BOARD: your whole rack, on the wall
A 200 x 169 mm board with 18 closed slots in three rows of six: a double rack of cams, a set of nuts and six quickdraws, each hanging through its own window so nothing tangles. Hang it from two hooks, or run a sling through the top slots and hang it from one hook, a car grab handle or a tree.

THE CRAG RING: grab your rack like a bag
A light 7 mm gear ring with 11 closed windows and a hand-size carry handle. Rack your cams, walk in carrying it by the handle, then clip the handle to a sling or a tree at the base. About 72 g.

AND FIVE MORE
The Rock Ring (13 windows) and Double Ring (20, draws on an inner row) for single racks; the Sport Board for 13 quickdraws; the 36 g Approach Bar for 7 draws in your pack; and the Pocket Bar for a pack lid or glovebox.

"NOT FOR CLIMBING" is debossed into every one, because they're organizers, not rated hardware.

EVERY DESIGN IS ORIGINAL
I modeled every piece from scratch around real carabiner gate openings and rack sizes, and I print every one myself. The files aren't for sale or download: the campaign is how I get printed ones to everyone else.

WHERE YOUR PLEDGE GOES
```

### Video (60–90 seconds, phone is fine)

1. 0–10 s: the problem. A cam sliding off a hook as a gear bin gets
   bumped; a rack spilling in the trunk.
2. 10–40 s: the Gear Board on the wall, loaded. Unclip a cam, clip it
   back, then pick the whole board up and shake it: nothing falls off.
   Then the Crag Ring: rack up, grab the handle, walk out the door, clip
   it to a tree at the crag. Quick cuts of the Approach Bar going into
   a pack and the Pocket Bar in a glovebox.
3. 40–60 s: you at the printer, a part coming off the bed. "Every design
   is original, printed here in Chicago."
4. 60–80 s: the ask. "$1,200 gets a real printer running and the first
   run to you. Early birds are up to 25% off for the first 50."

Kickstarter projects with a video fund at much higher rates. Real
footage of real parts is what backers trust.

## 3. Pre-launch (the most important 3 weeks)

1. **Submit the project for review** (1–3 days). Once approved, turn on
   the **pre-launch page**. Copy its URL into `SHOP.kickstarter.url` in
   `js/store-data.js`, leave `status: 'prelaunch'`, commit and push. The
   whole site now says "Follow on Kickstarter".
2. **Goal: 100+ followers before launch.** Followers get Kickstarter's
   launch-day email. About 20–30% of followers usually back in the first
   48 hours, and that first-day momentum is what gets you into
   Kickstarter's own recommendations.
3. Post the **site link** (https://alkalman123.github.io/RackHouse/kickstarter.html)
   rather than the raw Kickstarter link while in pre-launch. It previews
   with a proper image and explains things first.
4. Email everyone who used the "notify me" form (they'll be in
   rackhousesupplyco@gmail.com) the day the pre-launch page goes up, then
   again on launch day.

## 4. Sharing in climbing communities without getting banned

Most climbing communities **ban or limit self-promotion**. Read each
group's rules first, and lead with something useful.

- **Lead with the story, not the ask.** "I spent 40 prototypes on a gear
  board that never drops a carabiner. Here's what failed and why." Build
  photos, failed prototypes and the shake-it-upside-down test are welcome
  almost everywhere; ads aren't. Link the campaign at the end.
- **Disclose you're the maker** in every post.
- **Reddit:** r/climbing has strict self-promotion rules. Check the
  sidebar, and post in the weekly/self-promo thread if there is one.
  r/3Dprinting, r/functionalprint, r/tradclimbing and r/bouldering are
  better fits; post a build or "made this" photo of a racked-up Gear Board.
- **Facebook groups:** local climbing groups, gym groups, van-life and
  climbing-trip groups. Message an admin first and ask whether a
  one-time post about a local climber's project is OK.
- **Your gym:** ask to put a small poster with a QR code to the
  Kickstarter page by the front desk. Gyms often support local members.
- **Instagram/TikTok:** short clips of the parts in use (see
  `SOCIAL-MEDIA-KIT.md`), link in bio to the Kickstarter page.
- **Mountain Project forums:** the "For Sale/Free" and gear sections, if
  allowed.
- Never spam the same post across dozens of groups on one day. Two or
  three good posts a week beats twenty copies.

## 6. After it funds

- Kickstarter pays out about **14 days** after the campaign ends.
- Use Kickstarter's built-in **pledge manager / backer survey** to
  collect colors, tee sizes and addresses.
- Fulfillment uses the same pipeline as shop orders
  (`ORDER-INTAKE-AND-FULFILLMENT.md`). If volume beats one printer, route
  overflow to the drop-ship farm in Part 3b.
- Set `SHOP.kickstarter.status = 'funded'` and add a thank-you.
- **Taxes:** Kickstarter money is business income (you'll likely get a
  1099-K). Keep receipts for the printer, filament and packaging; they
  offset it. Talk to an accountant if it raises more than a few thousand.

## 7. If it doesn't fund

Nobody is charged and nothing is lost but time. The shop stays open,
you keep every follower's attention, and you relaunch with a lower goal
or better photos 4–8 weeks later. That's common, and it often works the
second time.
