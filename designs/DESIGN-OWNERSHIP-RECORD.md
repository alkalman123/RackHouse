## 3. Your creative decisions (human authorship)

The designs were built in Claude Code, an AI coding assistant, working
to your direction. Copyright attaches to the human creative choices, and
these were yours. Your instructions in this session, in order:

1. Rejected the first gear ring because it couldn't hold "an entire trad
   rack and a helmet", and asked for a straight version for extra gear
   and quickdraws (4 Oct 2026, before commit `bf71d6c`).
2. Rejected the next round because "it will not hold carabiners" and
   ordered 20 prototypes, "10 this style and 10 straight barred ones"
   (before commit `8710130`).
3. Reviewed all 20 and set the governing design rule: "It has to be a
   fully enclosed circle or box so that the carabiner does not fall out
   in any direction as this will be used in a storage closet, in the car,
   in the pack and at the crag." You kept only R05 and B06 and ordered a
   new round built on that rule (before commit `2b41b2a`).
4. Selected the production line from round 2: R12, R15, R20, B12, B14,
   B18, B20, and named B20 and R20 the two lead products (5 Oct 2026,
   before commit `2b55053`).

Those requirements (enclosed openings, use in closet/car/pack/crag, full
trad rack capacity) and the selection of seven designs out of 40 are the
creative choices that shaped what exists. The full conversation is in
your claude.ai account history; keep it. Your human contribution grows

## 4. AI-generated output

Anthropic's terms assign to the user whatever rights Anthropic has in
outputs ("we assign to you all our right, title, and interest, if any, in
Outputs"; check the current version at anthropic.com/legal). So nobody
else, including Anthropic, holds a claim to these designs. The open
question is narrower: how much copyright protection the US gives
AI-assisted work. The Copyright Office protects the human-authored parts:
your selection, arrangement and direction (section 3) and your own edits.
Disclose the AI assistance when you register.

## 5. Not copied from anyone

The first versions of the Gatekeeper, Rock Ring and Cup Cradle (Sept
2026) were someone else's non-commercial models. They were replaced on
4 Oct 2026 (commit `19ee25e`) with designs built from scratch from
measurements, and the old models were removed from every current file.
A geometry comparison of the old and new Cup Cradle files found only
about 2% of either surface within 1 mm of the other. The seven organizers
have no outside source at all: they come from your own prototype rounds.
Keep the old files out of every listing and never mark a Rackhouse design
as a remix of anything.

## 6. Make it stronger (recommended, in order)

1. **Register the copyright** at copyright.gov: about $45-65 online for
   the group of designs, with the STLs and renders as deposit copies.
   Registration is the formal proof, and in the US you need it before you
   can sue, and to claim statutory damages for copying after it.
2. **Trademark "RACKHOUSE"** at uspto.gov (about $350 per class; class 20
   for storage racks). This protects the brand on every product
   regardless of design copyright.
3. **Consider a design patent** for the Gear Board and Crag Ring, the
   strongest protection for a product's shape. US law allows filing up to
   one year after first public disclosure: the designs went public on
   4-5 Oct 2026, so the deadline is about **4 Oct 2027**. Many other
   countries allow no grace period. A patent attorney can quote it
   (typically $1,000-3,000 each).
4. **Keep everything**: this record, the archive, the conversation,
   prototype prints, receipts and dated photos of your printing work.
