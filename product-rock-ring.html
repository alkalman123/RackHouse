The Rock Ring

Portable edge lift block

$34.00

Hangboard training without a hangboard. The Rock Ring is a 10 cm T-profile block: pinch the top flange by its 19 mm edge, or flip it for the harder 11 mm edge, and lift a kettlebell, a loaded bag or plates hanging from a cord through the channel below. No door frame, no drilling, and it fits in a chalk bucket, so you can warm up at the crag or train in a hotel room.

Not climbing protection The Rock Ring is a training tool. It is not rated climbing equipment, an anchor or a belay point, and you should never hang body weight from it. Keep the weight low to the ground, use a cord or pin rated well above your load, and inspect the block before each session: retire it if you see cracks or layer separation. Finger pulleys strain easily, so warm up and add load gradually.

Material & care

Printed in PLA+, a plant-based bioplastic that's tougher and less brittle than standard PLA. It keeps its shape well at room temperature but will soften above roughly 50–60°C (122–140°F) — so it's happy in a gym bag or on a gear bench, less happy on a hot car dashboard in July.

  • Wipe clean with a dry or slightly damp cloth
  • Fine at the crag; just don't leave it baking in a parked car
  • Keep out of prolonged direct summer sun or hot vehicle interiors
  • Not dishwasher or microwave safe

How do I use it?

Loop a cord or sling through the channel and tie on your weight. Stand tall, grip the flange edge with the fingers of one hand (half-crimp or open hand), and lift the weight a few centimetres off the floor for a 7–10 second hold. Start well below what you can hang on a board, add weight gradually, and switch to the 11 mm edge when the 19 mm edge feels easy.

What weight should I use?

Most climbers start somewhere between 10 and 20 kg per hand on the 19 mm edge. The block isn't the weak link at those loads; your cord and knot are, so use rated accessory cord or a loading pin. Never use it overhead or as a hanging point for your body weight.

In use

A hangboard that fits in your pocket.

Run a cord through the channel, clip on a kettlebell, and lift from the edge: the same isometric hold you'd do on a hangboard, with exact control over load, one hand at a time. Pinch the 19 mm side to build up, flip to the 11 mm side when you're ready.
